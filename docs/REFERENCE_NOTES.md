# Reference observations

## How the references were inspected

The two reference images were supplied as attachments in the conversation and were
inspected visually there. The kit's `references/` and `before/` folders contained no
image files at the paths the brief names, so nothing was opened from disk. For the
"before" comparison I used my own browser captures of the running application taken
earlier in this session, at the same viewports and themes.

## Five concrete observations from the actual images

1. **Depth comes from stacked surfaces, not from shadow.** In the light reference the
   blue balance panel sits on top of a white card whose top edge is still visible above
   it, and a third dimmed card peeks above that. Three layers, each offset by roughly
   10 to 14 px. A single card with a drop shadow does not produce this.

2. **The figure-to-label ratio is extreme.** The balance reads at roughly 34 to 40 px
   against a 12 px label directly above it, and the cents are set lighter than the
   dollars. On the transfer screen the amount is larger still, well over 50 px, sitting
   directly on the blue atmosphere with a small "Available Balance" line beneath.

3. **Primary actions are paired pills of equal width.** One filled blue, one outlined,
   side by side and thumb height. Not a single button, and not a row of five icons.

4. **The dark reference separates layers by surface value, not by borders.** Near-black
   canvas, slightly lighter graphite cards, lighter again for overlays. Hairline borders
   are almost absent. Its accent appears only on the single next action, once per screen;
   everything else is greyscale.

5. **The dark assistant screen is a short vertical list, not a chat transcript.** A
   compact emblem, one line of greeting, then suggestion rows with small left-aligned
   labels and right-aligned values. Information density is high and the rows are quiet.

## What is deliberately not carried over

Logos, sample balances, card numbers, APY offers, referral banners, crypto features,
the acid-green accent, and the phone mockup frames. The brief asks for one blue identity
across both themes, so green stays a status colour only.
